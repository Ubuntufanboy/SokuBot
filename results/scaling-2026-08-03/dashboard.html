01  —  Learning rate decided everything

Two runs, same data, same architecture, same seed. The first used the learning rate tuned on short runs (5×10−4) and diverged at step 75,000: a 320k-step cosine schedule leaves the model near peak LR far longer than the 16k-step runs it was tuned on. Halving it to 2×10−4 fixed it outright.

lr 2e-4 — completed lr 5e-4 — diverged previous best (+0.520)

02  —  Why skill alone can lie

Skill is a ratio, so an exploding latent inflates both of its terms. During the divergence it rose from +0.35 to +0.69 while absolute prediction error got six times worse. The tell is identity: BatchNorm pins every latent dimension to unit variance, so two uncorrelated latents can differ by at most about 2.0. The failed run reached 7.68 — structurally impossible, and proof the normalisation had collapsed.

03  —  Data is the constraint, not compute

At a fixed 4,000 steps more data looks worthless — and at 10 hours it collapses, because the model sees under one epoch. Give both 16,000 steps and the picture inverts: 10 hours reaches +0.497 while 1 hour degrades to +0.178, having spent the extra compute memorising. That result is what justified collecting 200 hours.

04  —  Throughput

Three changes mattered. Sending frames to the GPU as uint8 instead of float32 cut PCIe traffic per batch from 308 MB to 77 MB and is bit-identical. Gating the training diagnostics removed a GPU synchronisation on every step. The rest was torch.compile. TF32, fused AdamW and batched SIGReg each measured as no-ops here.
